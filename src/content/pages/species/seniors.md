# Seniors: Shifting from Diagnosis to Monitoring

**Quick Answer** — In older animals the testing question changes. It is rarely "what disease does this animal have" and usually "is the disease I know about progressing, and has something new appeared". That shift makes **consistency** the most important property of a monitoring programme: the same markers, on the same platform, at a defined interval.

## Quick Answer

Diagnosis is a snapshot. Senior care is a series. The series only works if the method does not change mid-stream.

## The monitoring set

| Parameter | Why it is tracked | Frequency guide |
|---|---|---|
| Creatinine and urine specific gravity | Filtration and concentrating ability together | `[[ ]]` months |
| SDMA where creatinine is confounded | Muscle-mass-independent filtration | `[[ ]]` months |
| Urine protein:creatinine | Proteinuria is a treatment target, invisible to filtration markers | `[[ ]]` months |
| Blood pressure | Both cause and consequence of renal and cardiac disease | `[[ ]]` months |
| T4 in cats | Hyperthyroidism is common and treatable | `[[ ]]` months |
| NT-proBNP where murmur or signs exist | Cardiac load; interpretation with renal function | On indication |
| Inflammatory marker where chronic disease exists | Trend supports response assessment | On indication |

The first four rows are the ones most often skipped, and they are the ones that change management. A senior panel that reports creatinine and nothing else is measuring filtration and calling it a health check.

## Why consistency beats comprehensiveness

If a cat's creatinine is measured on one platform in March and a different one in September, the difference between the two values includes the bias between platforms. In an animal whose change over six months might be 15%, a 10–30% method bias is the whole signal.

The same principle applies to switching markers: tracking SDMA for a year and then creatinine for the next year produces a curve that cannot be interpreted.

## What a senior visit should decide

1. **Has anything changed since last time?** Requires comparable data.
2. **Is the known disease controlled?** Requires the marker that reflects it, not a general panel.
3. **Is there something new?** Weight, blood pressure, urine, and clinical examination find most of it — the laboratory confirms.
4. **When is the next measurement?** Written down now, not left to the next visit.

## Where senior testing goes wrong

- **Broad panels instead of targeted monitoring.** Cost without decision value.
- **Marker switching.** Trend becomes uninterpretable.
- **Skipping urine testing.** Proteinuria and concentrating ability are invisible to blood markers.
- **Skipping blood pressure.** Hypertension is common, silent and treatable.
- **Over-testing the stable animal.** Six-monthly is a default, not a ritual.
- **Treating an age-related value as disease.** Senior reference intervals matter, where they exist.

## Six traps

1. **Panel breadth substituting for monitoring consistency.**
2. **Ignoring the urine.** The commonest omission.
3. **No written next interval.**
4. **Treating an isolated value.** Trend first.
5. **Using a different platform each visit.** Bias enters the trend.
6. **Assuming normal filtration means normal kidney.** Proteinuria and hypertension are separate.

## The Author's Take

**Position.** Senior care is the segment where a vendor's value is least about assays and most about **record continuity** — and that is an argument for platform consistency that no brochure makes.

**Reasoning.** In a young animal, one test answers one question. In an older animal, the value is in the curve, and the curve is only as good as its comparability. A vendor that publishes its precision at monitoring concentrations, keeps reference intervals stable across lots, and warns about method switching is selling something more durable than a cartridge: it is selling the ability to know whether the animal is getting worse. Clinics that understand this become single-platform practices by choice, which is the most defensible form of customer retention available in this market.
