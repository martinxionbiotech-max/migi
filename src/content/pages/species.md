# Species Guides

Species is not a label on a reference interval; it changes which marker to use, what a result means, and which confounders apply. These guides are organised around the decisions clinicians actually make.

## By species

| Guide | Focus |
|---|---|

| [Cats](/species/cats/) | SAA dominance, retrovirus rules, thyroid–renal interaction |

| [Kittens](/species/kittens/) | Panleukopenia emergency, six-month retrovirus retest rule |
| [Seniors](/species/seniors/) | Monitoring consistency, urine and blood pressure as the skipped variables |

## The three species differences that change diagnoses

1. **Feline acute-phase biology.** SAA carries the feline response; CRP rises modestly. A canine-marker strategy applied to a cat understates inflammation.
2. **Maternal antibody.** It protects, blocks vaccination, and confounds antibody tests — differently for puppies and kittens, and with a defined retest window for kittens.
3. **Thyroid and kidney in cats.** Hyperthyroidism raises filtration and can mask azotaemia; treating it can unmask renal disease. T4 and renal parameters belong in the same plan.

## What each stage is really asking

| Stage | The question |
|---|---|
| Puppy / kitten | Is this animal infected now, and is it protected? |
| Adult | Does this episode need a specific treatment decision today? |
| Breeding animal | When, and is she pregnant? |
| Senior | Is the known disease progressing, and has something new appeared? |

## The Author's Take

**Position.** Species guides are where a manufacturer demonstrates whether it understands practice, and most do not, because species differences are usually handled by relabelling a canine page.

**Reasoning.** The three differences above are not cosmetic: each changes a diagnosis. A cat's inflammation read with CRP, a kitten declared FIV-positive at three months, or a hyperthyroid cat treated without checking renal function — these are errors that a well-written species page prevents and a relabelled canine page does not. The commercial corollary is that species content is hard to copy convincingly: a competitor can duplicate a menu table in an afternoon, but writing credible feline guidance requires people who work with cats.
