# Instructions for Use: Versions, Scope and What to Check

**Quick Answer** — An IFU is a controlled document, and its value depends on three fields: version, effective date, and the market it was written for. A copy without those is not usable in a registration dossier and is unsafe in practice, because procedure and performance claims change between revisions.

## What an IFU must state

| Section | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Intended use | Defines the claim; the basis of any regulatory or tender statement |
| Specimen type and handling | Where most field errors originate |
| Procedure | Including timing and temperature conditions |
| Performance | LoD/LoQ, precision, and any clinical performance stated |
| Limitations | Interference, hook effect, cross-reactivity |
| Storage and stability | Kit and sample |
| Version and effective date | Determines whether the document is current |

## Checking that the document matches your lot

1. **Compare versions.** An IFU older than the reagent lot should be replaced before use.
2. **Check the specimen type.** A method transferred to a new matrix without a revised IFU is out of scope.
3. **Check the performance figures.** If the IFU states a different LoD or precision from the page you relied on, the IFU governs the product and the difference should be reported.
4. **Check the language.** For registration, the market language is usually mandatory.

## Common discrepancies and what they mean

| Observation | Likely cause | Action |
|---|---|---|
| IFU older than the lot | Document not updated with the lot | Request current version |
| Performance figure differs from site | Site summarises across products | Confirm which applies; report |
| Specimen type differs from practice | Local adaptation without revision | Stop; confirm scope |
| Language mismatch | Market-specific versions | Request the correct market version |

## Six traps

1. **Using an IFU without a version check.** The most common documentation error.
2. **Treating the website as the controlled document.** It is a summary.
3. **Adapting procedure locally without a revised IFU.** Moves the assay out of its validated scope.
4. **Assuming one IFU covers all markets.** Registration status differs.
5. **Ignoring the limitations section.** It is where the failure modes are documented.
6. **Filing a superseded version in a dossier.** Invites a question that delays review.

## The Author's Take

**Position.** Publishing IFUs online, with version history, is the single most credibility-building act available to a diagnostic manufacturer — and it is usually avoided for reasons that do not survive inspection.

**Reasoning.** The worry is that a public IFU invites comparison and criticism. But the realistic alternative is worse: buyers receive a document by email, cannot verify its currency, and either delay a submission or use a superseded version. Both outcomes cost the vendor more than any criticism. A public library with versions and dates also demonstrates a functioning document control system, which is exactly what a distributor's quality audit is looking for — and it converts a support task into a competitive signal.
