# Reproduction Testing: Timing, Confirming, and the Order That Matters

**Quick Answer** — Breeding management uses two hormones for two different questions, and the most common failure is using them in the wrong order. Progesterone times ovulation and therefore insemination; relaxin confirms placental tissue later. A clinic that reverses the sequence will conclude one of the products is unreliable when the sequence, not the assay, was wrong.

## Quick Answer

Progesterone for the window. Relaxin for the answer. Ultrasound for the details.

## The two questions

| Question | Test | Timing | What it cannot do |
|---|---|---|---|
| When should she be bred? | Progesterone series | Periovulatory, every 48 h | Confirm pregnancy |
| Is she pregnant? | Relaxin | After the validated detection day | Time breeding |
| How many, and viable? | Ultrasound | Later in gestation | Predict ovulation timing |

## The progesterone series, done properly

1. **Start at first detection of oestrus**, not on a calendar day.
2. **Sample at a fixed interval**, commonly 48 hours.
3. **Continue until the threshold is bracketed** — one value below, one above, same platform.
4. **Time insemination from the bracket**, not from a single reading.
5. **Stop once well past ovulation** — the luteal plateau carries no timing information.

## Why pseudopregnancy matters

An elevated progesterone confirms a luteal phase, which occurs whether or not the bitch conceived. Relaxin is the test that distinguishes the two, and it is the reason a negative relaxin at the correct interval is genuinely informative: it changes the conversation from "wait and see" to "this is hormonal".

## The repeat breeder

Before investigating infertility, establish whether the cycle was actually managed:

| Question | If the answer is unclear |
|---|---|
| Was ovulation confirmed by a series? | The record, not the bitch, is the first problem |
| Was insemination timed to the bracket? | Timing error is a common cause of "infertility" |
| Was the same platform used throughout? | A platform change can mimic a stall or a rise |
| Was relaxin run at the validated interval? | An early negative is uninterpretable |

## Monitoring an established pregnancy

Progesterone is not a pregnancy-monitoring test in routine use, and serial progesterone in a confirmed pregnancy should follow a specific clinical indication rather than habit. Where supplementary progesterone is being considered, the decision belongs to the clinician and the reproductive guidance in use, not to a threshold table.

## Six traps

1. **Timing breeding with relaxin.** Wrong hormone, wrong question.
2. **A single progesterone value.** The curve carries the information.
3. **Irregular sampling intervals.** Slope interpretation collapses.
4. **Changing platform mid-cycle.** Bias shift mimics biological change.
5. **Interpreting an early negative relaxin as "not pregnant".** Retest at the validated interval.
6. **Working up infertility without cycle records.** The commonest missed cause is timing.

## The Author's Take

**Position.** Reproduction is the segment where a vendor's best product is a **protocol**, and the clinics that adopt one become the most loyal customers in the menu.

**Reasoning.** Progesterone and relaxin are both commodities; several suppliers have them. What a breeder or a reproduction clinic cannot buy easily is consistency — the same schedule, the same interpretation rule, the same record format across staff and seasons. Supplying that turns two assays into a managed programme, reduces the support burden dramatically (most "test failure" calls in this segment are timing calls), and generates predictable repeat testing. It is also the rare case where a vendor can improve outcomes without needing better data: the data is already good enough; the workflow is what fails.
