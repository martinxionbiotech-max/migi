## Quick Answer

Canine distemper is diagnosed on the combination of compatible clinical signs, antigen or nucleic-acid detection, and exclusion of other causes. Antibody testing answers a different question — immune status — and cannot distinguish infection from vaccination in a previously vaccinated dog. The most common error with this virus is treating a positive antibody result in a vaccinated dog as evidence of infection, and the second most common is excluding distemper on a single negative antigen test taken from an inadequate sample.

## What the two tests measure

| Test | Sample | Answers | Cannot answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| CDV antigen | Conjunctival swab, respiratory secretions, urine, serum (assay-dependent) | Is viral antigen present now | Whether the dog is immune |
| CDV antibody | Serum | Has the dog been exposed or vaccinated | Whether the disease is active |

In a vaccinated population, antibody is expected and is the point of vaccination. It is a statement about immune history, not about current infection.

## Why antigen testing is the diagnostic anchor

Distemper is a multi-system disease with a clinical picture that overlaps with respiratory, neurological and gastrointestinal conditions. Antigen detection in an appropriate sample is the finding that anchors the diagnosis, and its reliability depends heavily on sample choice and timing:

- **Conjunctival or respiratory samples** are most useful in the early, mucosal phase.
- **Urine** can remain positive later, particularly with renal involvement.
- **Sample quality matters more than assay sensitivity** — a poorly collected swab produces a false negative regardless of the platform.

## The antibody question, handled honestly

Antibody testing has two legitimate uses:

1. **Immune status assessment** — deciding whether a dog needs vaccination, particularly in breeding or group-housing settings.
2. **Supporting a diagnosis in an unvaccinated dog** with compatible signs, where seroconversion or a high titre is meaningful.

It has one illegitimate use: diagnosing infection in a vaccinated dog. Vaccine-induced antibody and infection-induced antibody are, for practical purposes, indistinguishable on a binding assay. Claiming otherwise — or implying that a quantitative titre separates them — is the kind of claim that fails the first technical review.

## Interpretation matrix

| Antigen | Antibody | Picture | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| Positive | Any | Compatible signs | Treat as distemper; isolate |
| Negative | Negative | Strong suspicion | Repeat with a better sample; consider PCR |
| Negative | Positive | Vaccinated adult, mild signs | Distemper less likely; look for other causes |
| Negative | Positive | Unvaccinated puppy with neurological signs | Cannot exclude; antigen sampling may be past the window |

## Six traps

1. **Reading antibody as infection.** The dominant error.
2. **One negative antigen on a poor sample.** Repeat with conjunctival or urine sampling.
3. **Testing only at the neurological stage.** Antigen may be undetectable while disease is advanced.
4. **Ignoring vaccination history.** It changes the meaning of every antibody result.
5. **Missing antigen excess.** Severe cases can read low; dilution is the check.
6. **Treating a quantitative titre as a severity marker.** It is not.

## The Author's Take

**Position.** The page should lead with antigen, and should say plainly that antibody cannot distinguish vaccination from infection — because that single sentence protects the whole menu from a credibility failure.

**Reasoning.** Distemper is a disease where clinicians want a definitive answer quickly, and a positive antibody in a vaccinated dog is often taken as one. When that happens, the assay gets blamed for the misreading. Publishing the limitation converts a predictable complaint into a documented interpretation rule, and it also positions the antigen assay as the diagnostic product while the antibody assay stays where it belongs: immunity management. That split is commercially cleaner, because the two tests then serve two different buyers instead of arguing with each other.
