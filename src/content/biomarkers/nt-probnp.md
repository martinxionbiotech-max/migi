## Quick Answer

NT-proBNP is a stable fragment released when ventricular myocardium is stretched, and its most defensible clinical use in small animal practice is **ruling out** cardiac causes of respiratory distress, not screening for early heart disease. A low value makes heart failure unlikely; a high value is compatible with heart failure but also with renal disease, pulmonary hypertension, sepsis and age. The test earns its place in the dyspnoeic patient, in the grey zone between "cardiac or respiratory", and it disappoints when used as a wellness screen.

## What it measures

Ventricular myocytes release a precursor that is cleaved into the active hormone BNP and the inactive fragment NT-proBNP. The fragment has a longer half-life and is more stable in circulation, which is why assays target it. Concentration reflects wall stress and volume/pressure load, not contractility, not ischaemia, and not the presence of any particular disease.

Two consequences:

1. **It is a physiological signal, not a diagnosis.** Anything that stretches the ventricle raises it.
2. **It moves on a timescale of hours.** It is useful for acute presentations and less useful for tracking slow chronic change.

## Where the test changes a decision

| Clinical scenario | What NT-proBNP contributes |
|---|---|
| Dyspnoeic cat or dog, cardiac vs respiratory unknown | A low value substantially reduces the probability of heart failure and supports treating for respiratory disease |
| Murmur with equivocal radiographs | Supports or argues against cardiac contribution before echocardiography |
| Known heart disease, new respiratory signs | Helps separate decompensation from concurrent airway disease |
| Pre-anaesthetic assessment with a murmur | Context for referral decision, not a substitute for imaging |
| Asymptomatic screening | Low yield; false positives generate unnecessary investigation |

The first row is the strongest use and the one worth building the page around. In a cat with rapid, shallow breathing, the practical question is whether to start diuretics and oxygen immediately or to investigate the airway and pleura — and NT-proBNP is one of the few point-of-care tests that moves that decision before imaging is available.

## Why quantitative

A qualitative or cut-off-only result collapses the informative middle of the distribution, and the middle is where the clinical question lives: values near the decision limit are exactly the ones that require interpretation against renal function, age, and the clinical picture.

## Interpretation against confounders

| Confounder | Effect | Practical handling |
|---|---|---|
| Renal disease / azotaemia | Reduced clearance raises values | Interpret alongside creatinine/SDMA; a high value with renal disease is not diagnostic of heart failure |
| Age | Values rise with age in some populations | Use age-appropriate interval where available |
| Pulmonary hypertension | Ventricular strain raises values | Consider echo for right-sided assessment |
| Sepsis / systemic inflammation | Can raise values | Interpret with the acute-phase picture |
| Breed | Reported differences in some breeds | Note where relevant rather than generalising |

The renal row explains most discordant results in practice: a cat with chronic kidney disease and a high NT-proBNP is a genuinely difficult case, and the test cannot resolve it alone. Saying so is more useful than publishing a clean cut-off.

## Six traps

1. **Screening asymptomatic animals.** Poor yield; false positives drive unnecessary echo referrals.
2. **Applying canine thresholds to cats.** Different biology, different limits.
3. **Ignoring renal function.** The most common cause of a misleading high value.
4. **Treating a grey-zone value as a diagnosis.** Grey zone means "image the patient".
5. **Using it to exclude early or mild disease.** A normal value does not exclude mild hypertrophy or early dysfunction.
6. **Comparing platforms mid-case.** Calibration differs; trends across systems are uninterpretable.

## The Author's Take

**Position.** Sell NT-proBNP for the dyspnoeic patient and say plainly that it is a poor screening test — the narrower claim converts better than the broad one.

**Reasoning.** A wellness-screening claim invites exactly the cases where the test performs worst: young asymptomatic animals with murmurs, geriatric cats with renal disease, and anxious patients with high respiratory effort. Each produces a false positive or an ambiguous value, each generates a referral, and the clinician concludes the assay is unreliable. The dyspnoea claim is different: prevalence of heart failure is high, the clinical question is binary and urgent, and a negative result genuinely changes the plan. A vendor that narrows its own claim is doing something buyers notice, because everybody else is widening theirs — and in a category where over-claiming is the norm, restraint reads as competence.
