## Quick Answer

Feline total T4 is the first-line test for hyperthyroidism and works well in cats with overt disease — but a normal result does not exclude it. A meaningful proportion of hyperthyroid cats, particularly early in the course, sit inside the reference interval, and concurrent non-thyroidal illness can push T4 down further. The test is best understood as a screening step with a known false-negative rate, not as a definitive rule-out.

## What it measures

Total thyroxine reflects thyroid hormone production plus binding. In cats, hyperthyroidism is usually caused by functional thyroid adenoma or hyperplasia, and T4 rises with the autonomous production. Two confounders dominate feline interpretation:

1. **Non-thyroidal illness** suppresses T4, and cats with hyperthyroidism are frequently old and frequently have concurrent disease — chronic kidney disease being the classic example.
2. **Stress and sampling** can shift values modestly, though far less than illness does in the opposite direction.

## The diagnostic problem in one table

| T4 | Clinical picture | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| High | Weight loss, polyphagia, palpable thyroid | Hyperthyroidism; treat |
| High-normal | Compatible signs | Suspicious; repeat in 2–4 weeks or pursue further testing |
| Mid-normal | Compatible signs | Hyperthyroidism not excluded; repeat testing is the standard next step |
| Low | Compatible signs, concurrent illness | Illness suppression possible; reassess after treating the other disease |
| Low | No compatible signs | Hyperthyroidism unlikely |

The third row is the one that matters. In early or mild disease, a cat can sit comfortably inside the reference interval, and a single "normal" result is the most common reason a diagnosis is delayed by months.

## Why quantitative and why repeated

| Question | Single value | Serial values |
|---|---|---|
| Is T4 elevated? | Above/below interval | Trend over weeks clarifies borderline cats |
| Is this illness suppression? | Not distinguishable | Rise after illness resolves is informative |
| Is treatment adequate? | One data point | Trend against dose adjustments |

Serial measurement is not a marketing suggestion; in feline thyroid disease it is the diagnostic method for the equivocal case.

## Where free T4 fits

Free T4 by equilibrium dialysis is the usual second-line test when total T4 is equivocal. Its limitation is reciprocal: it improves sensitivity in early disease but loses specificity, because non-thyroidal illness can raise free T4 in euthyroid cats. The practical rule is that free T4 is useful when the clinical picture strongly suggests hyperthyroidism and total T4 is normal — not as a general screen.

## Monitoring treatment

- **Methimazole:** T4 measured at a consistent interval after dosing; the target is a mid-normal value with clinical improvement, not the lowest value in range.
- **Radioiodine / thyroidectomy:** T4 falls, sometimes into the low range transiently; monitor for hypothyroidism.
- **Renal function:** treating hyperthyroidism can unmask or worsen azotaemia, because increased glomerular filtration was masking reduced renal function. T4 and renal parameters should be interpreted together, before and after treatment.

## Six traps

1. **Excluding hyperthyroidism on one normal T4.** The most consequential error in feline endocrinology.
2. **Ignoring concurrent illness.** Illness suppresses T4 in exactly the population that develops hyperthyroidism.
3. **Using free T4 as a screen.** Poor specificity in sick cats.
4. **Missing the renal interaction.** Treatment can unmask azotaemia; monitor both.
5. **Comparing laboratories or platforms over time.** Calibration and method differ.
6. **Treating a high-normal value without clinical correlation.** Palpable gland, weight trend and signs matter.

**Position.** The single most useful sentence a vendor can publish on this page is "a normal total T4 does not exclude hyperthyroidism — repeat testing is expected, not a sign of assay failure."

**Reasoning.** Feline hyperthyroidism is a diagnosis clinicians are usually confident about, and the test most often "disagrees" in the early cases where the diagnosis is actually being made on clinical grounds.
