## Quick Answer

Relaxin is produced by the placenta and is detectable in maternal plasma from roughly the time of implantation, which makes it the most practical way to answer a question progesterone cannot: **is this dog pregnant, or is this a pseudopregnancy?** It does not tell you when to breed — that is progesterone's job — and it cannot be interpreted before the validated detection window, which is the most common reason it "fails" in practice.

## What it measures

Relaxin is a peptide hormone secreted by the developing placental tissue. Its presence in plasma is therefore evidence that placental tissue exists, not merely that ovulation or a luteal phase occurred. That distinction is the whole value of the test:

| Question | Progesterone | Relaxin |
|---|---|---|
| Has ovulation occurred? | Yes | No |
| Is the luteal phase present? | Yes | No |
| Is there placental tissue? | No | Yes |
| Is this pseudopregnancy? | Cannot answer | Can help distinguish |
| When should I breed? | Yes | No |

Pseudopregnancy is common in bitches and produces behavioural and physical signs indistinguishable from early pregnancy to the owner. A negative relaxin result in a bitch with mammary development and nesting behaviour, at the correct interval after oestrus, is genuinely useful information — it changes the conversation from "wait and see" to "this is hormonal, and here is what to expect."

## Timing: the failing point of the test

Relaxin cannot be detected before placental tissue is established. Testing too early produces a negative result that is then read as "not pregnant", when the correct interpretation is "too early to know".

A defensible protocol:

1. Establish ovulation timing separately (progesterone series), so the interval is measured from a known reference point rather than from the first day of bleeding.
2. Test at or after the validated earliest detection day.
3. If negative and clinical suspicion persists, retest after the recommended interval before concluding.
4. Record the day of testing relative to ovulation in the record; it is the only way to audit a discordant result later.

## Interpretation

| Result | Timing | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| Positive | After detection window | Placental tissue present; pregnancy likely — confirm with ultrasound for viability and count |
| Negative | After detection window, suspicion persists | Retest per interval; consider early embryonic loss |
| Negative | Before detection window | Uninterpretable — repeat at the correct interval |
| Positive | After whelping | Retained placental tissue or recent pregnancy; clinical correlation |

## Pairing with progesterone, correctly

The two tests are commonly sold together but frequently used in the wrong order. The correct sequence is:

- **Progesterone first**, to time ovulation and breeding.
- **Relaxin later**, to confirm pregnancy once placental tissue is established.

Using relaxin to time breeding is a category error: by the time relaxin is detectable, the fertile window has long closed. A clinic that buys both tests but reverses the sequence will conclude that one of them is unreliable, when in fact the sequence was wrong.

## Six traps

1. **Testing before the detection window.** The most common cause of a false negative.
2. **Using relaxin to time breeding.** Different question, different hormone.
3. **Using relaxin to estimate litter size or gestational age.** Not validated for that purpose.
4. **Concluding "not pregnant" on a single early negative.** Retest at the correct interval.
5. **Ignoring embryonic loss.** A positive that later turns negative is information, not an assay failure.
6. **Comparing across platforms.** Cut-offs differ; a value from another assay is not transferable.

## The Author's Take

**Position.** Relaxin's most commercially valuable use is not "pregnancy confirmation" — it is **resolving pseudopregnancy**, because that is the case where the owner has already decided something and the clinician needs a fact.

**Reasoning.** Pregnancy confirmation is usually achieved by ultrasound a short time later, so relaxin competes with a test the clinic already owns. Pseudopregnancy is different: ultrasound shows no fetuses but cannot easily distinguish "too early" from "not pregnant", the owner is emotionally invested, and the conversation can go on for weeks. A blood-based answer that arrives earlier than imaging, with a defensible cut-off, ends that conversation. Vendors who market relaxin as a pregnancy test compete with ultrasound; vendors who market it as a pseudopregnancy resolver complement it — and complement is the easier sale.
