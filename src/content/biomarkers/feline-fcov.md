## Quick Answer

Feline coronavirus is extremely common; feline infectious peritonitis is rare. That asymmetry is the whole clinical problem: in a cattery, most cats are FCoV antibody positive, and the vast majority never develop FIP. A positive antibody result therefore says almost nothing about FIP, and a positive antigen result in serum says only that virus is present somewhere — not that it has mutated, disseminated, or caused disease. FIP remains a clinical diagnosis built from signalment, effusion analysis, imaging and, where available, histopathology.

## What the tests measure

| Test | Sample | Answers | Cannot answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| FCoV antibody | Serum | Has the cat met FCoV | Whether this cat has FIP |
| FCoV antigen | Serum, faeces, effusion | Is virus present | Whether disease is FIP |
| (PCR, referral) | Effusion, tissue | Is viral RNA present, and of what type | Clinical significance without context |

## Why the antibody test is the most misused test in feline medicine

FCoV spreads readily among cats in multi-cat households and catteries. Seroprevalence in such populations can be very high. Most infections are self-limiting or subclinical; a small proportion develop FIP, usually associated with a viral mutation and an inadequate immune response.

The consequence: **a positive FCoV antibody test is a statement about exposure, not about disease.** Diagnosing FIP from a titre is the classic error, and it leads to euthanasia of healthy cats — a decision that is irreversible and, in some cases, unnecessary given modern antiviral options.

## What actually supports an FIP diagnosis

A composite, and the effusion — when present — is the most informative sample:

| Finding | Weight |
|---|---|
| Young cat, purebred or from a multi-cat environment | Supports |
| Ascites or pleural effusion, high protein, low albumin:globulin ratio | Strongly supports |
| Rivalta test positive on effusion | Supports |
| Pyogranulomatous inflammation on effusion cytology | Supports |
| Coronavirus antigen or RNA in effusion | Strongly supports |
| Serum antibody titre | Almost no diagnostic value alone |
| Histopathology with immunohistochemistry | Definitive |

The non-effusive (dry) form is harder: it presents as pyrexia of unknown origin, weight loss, uveitis or neurological signs, and no single test confirms it.

## Where point-of-care testing fits

1. **Effusion antigen testing** — the highest-value use, because it is the sample most likely to reflect the disease process.
2. **Serum antigen** — supports the presence of virus in a cat with compatible signs, but must not be read as confirmation.
3. **Antibody** — useful for population management decisions in catteries (identifying naive cats), not for individual diagnosis.

## Six traps

1. **Diagnosing FIP from an antibody titre.** The most consequential error in feline infectious disease.
2. **Reading serum antigen as confirmation of FIP.** Virus presence is not disease.
3. **Ignoring effusion analysis.** Protein, A:G ratio and Rivalta carry more weight than any single serology result.
4. **Applying cattery seroprevalence to a household cat.** Different prior probability.
5. **Missing the dry form because no effusion is present.** Fever of unknown origin and uveitis deserve consideration.
6. **Treating a positive result as a death sentence.** Modern antiviral therapy has changed the prognosis conversation; the diagnosis should be as solid as possible first.

## The Author's Take

**Position.** The most valuable sentence on this page is "a positive coronavirus antibody test does not diagnose FIP" — and a vendor should print it more prominently than any sensitivity figure.

**Reasoning.** FIP is the disease where a laboratory result most often ends a cat's life. A titre taken casually, in a young cat from a multi-cat household, can produce a euthanasia decision on the strength of a result that describes exposure rather than disease. Any vendor selling into this space inherits that risk, and the only defensible response is to make the limitation impossible to miss and to point clinicians towards effusion analysis. This is not only ethics: the vendors who are trusted on FIP are the ones clinicians consult when the case is genuinely difficult, which is where the durable relationship is built.
