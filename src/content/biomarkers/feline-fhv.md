## Quick Answer

Feline herpesvirus is a virus of latency, and that single biological fact governs everything about testing it. Antigen detection in an acutely affected cat with conjunctivitis or respiratory signs is meaningful; antibody detection is almost meaningless in a vaccinated population; and a negative test in a latently infected cat that has reactivated is a timing problem, not an assay failure. The most useful question the test answers is "is FHV contributing to this episode right now", not "has this cat ever met FHV".

## What the tests measure

| Test | Sample | Answers | Cannot answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| FHV antigen | Conjunctival or oropharyngeal swab | Is virus present in this episode | Whether the cat will reactivate later |
| FHV antibody | Serum | Prior exposure or vaccination | Whether the current episode is FHV |

## Latency: why this virus is different

After acute infection, FHV establishes latency in the trigeminal ganglion. Reactivation follows stress — parturition, rehoming, illness, corticosteroid therapy — and produces recurrent episodes, often with ocular signs dominating. Two consequences for testing:

1. **A positive antigen result in a latently infected cat during a stress episode is genuine and relevant** — it identifies the cause of this episode.
2. **A negative antigen result between episodes is expected**, because the virus is not being shed.

This is why timing, not sensitivity, is the limiting factor in FHV diagnosis.

## Why antibody testing is weak here

Vaccination is widespread, and vaccine-induced antibody cannot be distinguished from infection-induced antibody on a binding assay. A positive result in an owned adult cat is nearly uninformative. Antibody's legitimate use is population-level immunity assessment in catteries, not individual diagnosis.

## Where antigen testing changes management

- **Recurrent conjunctivitis or keratitis** in a cat with a history of respiratory disease: FHV is a leading cause, and identifying it changes management towards antiviral therapy and stress reduction rather than repeated antibiotic courses.
- **Corneal ulceration with dendritic or geographic patterns**: FHV supports antiviral rather than antibacterial-only treatment.
- **Outbreak in a cattery or shelter**: same-day antigen detection supports isolation and cohorting.

## Interpretation matrix

| Antigen | Clinical picture | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| Positive | Acute conjunctivitis, respiratory signs | FHV contributing; consider antiviral therapy |
| Positive | Chronic recurrent ocular signs | Reactivation likely; manage stress and treat |
| Negative | Acute signs, vaccinated cat | FHV not excluded; sample site and timing matter |
| Negative | Between episodes, no signs | Expected in a latent carrier; not a rule-out |

## Six traps

1. **Excluding FHV on a negative swab between episodes.** Latency means no shedding between episodes.
2. **Using antibody to diagnose.** Uninformative in vaccinated populations.
3. **Sampling the wrong site.** Conjunctival swabs are more useful than nasal alone for ocular disease.
4. **Missing the herpes–cornea association.** Dendritic ulcers are a clinical signal, not a laboratory one.
5. **Corticosteroid use without considering reactivation.** A treatment decision, not a test result.
6. **Treating every positive as a new infection.** Recurrent episodes in carriers are expected.

## The Author's Take

**Position.** FHV's clinical value is concentrated in ophthalmology, and a vendor that markets it as a general respiratory panel item misses the one specialty where it changes treatment.

**Reasoning.** General respiratory testing rarely changes management — most cats are treated supportively and the owner declines the test. But an ophthalmologist or a GP facing a non-healing corneal ulcer has a genuine decision: antiviral versus antibacterial, and whether to use a topical steroid (a decision that can worsen a herpetic ulcer). In that moment, same-day FHV antigen detection is worth paying for, and the clinician already knows why. Positioning FHV for ocular disease is narrower, more defensible, and it reaches a buyer with a higher willingness to pay than the general respiratory market.
