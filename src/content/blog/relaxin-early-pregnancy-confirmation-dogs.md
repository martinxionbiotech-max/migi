> **TL;DR:** Relaxin is the earliest blood-based confirmation of canine pregnancy, becoming detectable around 21–28 days after ovulation — as early as 25 days in the original studies, and 95–100% reliable by day 30. Its real clinical value is two-fold: it's pregnancy-specific (so it distinguishes true pregnancy from false pregnancy), and it confirms a "yes" weeks before whelping. The catch is timing — a negative result before the window proves nothing.

Of all the reproductive biomarkers, relaxin has the cleanest clinical story: it's produced by the placenta, it's essentially absent in non-pregnant dogs, and its appearance in blood is a reliable early signal of pregnancy. That specificity is exactly why it's become the standard blood test for early canine pregnancy confirmation.

## What Relaxin Is and Why It's Specific

Relaxin is a hormone produced by the developing placenta in pregnant bitches. The key property that makes it a diagnostic gold standard: **it's pregnancy-specific.** A non-pregnant dog produces essentially none, which means a positive result is strong evidence of a real pregnancy — and, just as usefully, a negative result helps rule out false pregnancy (pseudopregnancy), a common source of confusion in intact bitches.

## The Detection Window

Relaxin's timing is the whole story, so it's worth being precise:

- **~21–28 days after ovulation** — the placenta develops and relaxin becomes detectable.
- **As early as 25 days** — the original clinical study using the ReproCHEK ELISA reported detection in maternal blood at this point (Buff et al., 2001).
- **After day 28** — detection becomes more reliable.
- **Day 30 post-ovulation** — a validated test detects relaxin in **95–100% of pregnant dogs**.

The progression matters because of what it means for negative results. Run a relaxin test too early — before the placenta has developed enough to produce detectable hormone — and a negative result tells you nothing. It may simply be too soon.

## Reading a Negative Result

The single most important clinical lesson with relaxin: **a negative result is only meaningful after the detection window.**

- **Negative before ~21–28 days:** inconclusive. Re-test a week later.
- **Negative after day 28–30:** strong evidence of non-pregnancy.

This timing sensitivity is the reason a well-run breeding program schedules the relaxin test deliberately — around day 28–30 post-ovulation, when a negative is conclusive and a positive is reliable — rather than running it as soon as the breeder is impatient for an answer.

## Distinguishing Real Pregnancy from False Pregnancy

False pregnancy is one of the more frustrating presentations in canine reproduction — a bitch shows mammary development, nesting behaviour, and even lactation, but isn't actually pregnant.

Relaxin cuts through this cleanly. Because it's placenta-derived, a false pregnancy produces no relaxin. A positive relaxin confirms a real pregnancy even when clinical signs are ambiguous; a negative relaxin, in a dog showing pseudopregnancy signs, confirms there's no pregnancy to manage.

This is a concrete, daily-use case for the test, and it's one of the reasons relaxin earns its place in the breeding toolkit alongside ultrasound.

## Relaxin vs Ultrasound

The two tools answer slightly different versions of the same question:

| Tool | What it confirms | Timing | Additional value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Relaxin (blood) | Pregnancy yes/no | ~21–28 days | Pregnancy-specific, distinguishes false pregnancy |
| Ultrasound | Pregnancy + viability | ~21–25 days onward | Visual confirmation, foetal count, viability |

Relaxin is the earliest reliable blood "yes." Ultrasound adds the visual layer — viability, litter size, and how the pregnancy is progressing. They're complementary, not competing: relaxin for the earliest confirmation, ultrasound for the detail.

## A Practical Caution: Positive Doesn't Always Mean Viable

One nuance keeps relaxin from being over-read. Relaxin remains detectable throughout gestation and can persist into the post-partum period — and after foetal loss, levels may take time to fall.

The implication: a positive relaxin confirms that a pregnancy *was* established, but it is not, on its own, proof that it remains viable at that moment. When viability is in question — for example, after a suspected resorption — pair a positive relaxin with ultrasound to see what's actually happening.

## Application & Commercial Angle

Who should care: breeders and clinics confirming pregnancy early. Relaxin gives a same-visit confirmation that supports rebooking and monitoring.

In practice, it extends the breeding relationship beyond ovulation timing — the clinic stays involved through pregnancy confirmation and the decisions that follow.

## FAQ

### How early can relaxin detect pregnancy in a dog?

Relaxin becomes detectable in blood around 21–28 days after ovulation. The clinical literature reports detection as early as 25 days (Buff et al., 2001), with more reliable detection after day 28 — and by day 30, a validated test detects it in 95–100% of pregnant dogs.

### How reliable is a relaxin test?

By day 30 post-ovulation, a validated relaxin test detects pregnancy in 95–100% of pregnant dogs. A single negative result before that window is not conclusive — timing matters, because relaxin only becomes detectable once the placenta develops.

### Can a negative relaxin test rule out pregnancy?

Not before **~21–28 days post-ovulation**. If the test is run too early, a negative result may simply mean the placenta hasn't developed enough relaxin yet; a negative in the correct window is strong evidence of non-pregnancy.

### Does relaxin distinguish a real pregnancy from a false pregnancy?

Yes — this is one of relaxin's main clinical values. Relaxin is produced by the placenta, so it's essentially absent in false pregnancy (pseudopregnancy). A positive relaxin confirms a real pregnancy; a negative in a dog showing pseudopregnancy signs helps rule it out.

### How does relaxin compare to ultrasound for pregnancy confirmation?

Two tools, two jobs: relaxin is a blood test confirming pregnancy from **~21–28 days**; ultrasound visualises gestational structures from **~21–25 days** and also assesses viability and litter size. They're complementary.

### Can relaxin remain detectable after a failed pregnancy?

Yes — **1 positive relaxin is not proof of ongoing viability**: relaxin can remain detectable throughout gestation and after foetal loss, so pair it with ultrasound when viability is in question.

## Key Takeaways

1. Relaxin is the earliest blood-based confirmation of pregnancy, detectable ~21–28 days post-ovulation.
2. Detection reaches 95–100% by day 30 — schedule the test deliberately, not on impatience.
3. A negative result before the window is inconclusive; after day 28–30 it's strong evidence of non-pregnancy.
4. Relaxin distinguishes real pregnancy from false pregnancy — **1 placental signal**, its most useful daily application.
5. A positive relaxin confirms **1 thing**: pregnancy was established — ultrasound is still needed for viability.
