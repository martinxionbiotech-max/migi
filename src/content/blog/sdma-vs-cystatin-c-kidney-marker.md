# SDMA vs. Cystatin C: Which Kidney Marker to Choose

> **TL;DR** — Creatinine is a late, muscle-mass-dependent kidney marker. SDMA and cystatin C both fix that problem: they rise at ~25–40% function loss (creatinine waits for ~60–75%) and ignore muscle mass. SDMA has the stronger evidence base and is the IRIS staging standard; cystatin C is a valuable independent confirmatory marker. Most clinics use SDMA first and add cystatin C when the picture is unclear.

---

## In Plain Terms

Think of the kidney as a filter and creatinine as a water-level gauge. The gauge only reads "danger" when the tank is nearly full — by the time creatinine rises, most of the filter's capacity is already gone. SDMA and cystatin C are more like flow meters: they register the rate slowing down the moment filtration drops, at 25–40% loss instead of 60–75%. That's the difference between catching a clog early and discovering it after the flood.

---

## The Creatinine Problem, Restated

For decades, creatinine was the only routine way to assess kidney function in practice. It's cheap, familiar, and on every chemistry panel. But it has two structural flaws that have quietly cost a lot of early diagnoses:

1. **It's late.** Creatinine doesn't budge until roughly 60–75% of nephron function is gone. By the time creatinine is high, the kidney is already most of the way to failure.
2. **It's muscle-mass dependent.** A cachectic geriatric cat produces less creatinine, so a failing kidney can hide behind a "normal" creatinine. The sicker and thinner the patient, the less reliable the marker.

The result: too many CKD cases present at IRIS Stage 3 or 4, when the window for meaningful intervention has narrowed.

SDMA and cystatin C are the two markers designed to fix this.

---

## What Is SDMA?

**Symmetric dimethylarginine (SDMA)** is a methylated amino acid produced at a constant rate by all nucleated cells. It's cleared almost entirely by glomerular filtration, with no meaningful reabsorption or secretion — which is what makes it a near-ideal GFR marker.

| Property | SDMA |
|---|---|
| Normal range (dog & cat) | 0–14 µg/dL |
| Suspicious / borderline | 15–18 µg/dL (persistent) |
| Consistent with CKD | >18 µg/dL |
| Rises at | ~25–40% function loss |
| Muscle-mass dependent? | No |

Two things make SDMA genuinely different from creatinine:

- **It rises early.** SDMA increases when only a quarter to a third of kidney function is lost — often a year or more before creatinine moves.
- **It's muscle-mass independent.** A wasted senior cat and a muscular young dog with the same kidney function will have the same SDMA. Creatinine can't make that claim.

A persistent SDMA above 14 µg/dL with a normal creatinine supports **Stage 1 CKD** — the earliest, most treatable stage. For more on SDMA specifically, see [Early Detection of Chronic Kidney Disease: The Role of SDMA](/blog/early-detection-chronic-kidney-disease-sdma-dogs-cats).

---

## What Is Cystatin C?

**Cystatin C** is a low-molecular-weight protein produced by all nucleated cells and freely filtered at the glomerulus. Like SDMA, it's muscle-mass-independent and rises earlier than creatinine.

| Property | Cystatin C (approx.) |
|---|---|
| Normal range (dog) | 0.5–1.2 mg/L |
| Normal range (cat) | 0.6–1.8 mg/L |
| Rises at | ~25–40% function loss (similar to SDMA) |
| Muscle-mass dependent? | No |

There's one important caveat with cystatin C: **the reference interval is assay-dependent.** Unlike SDMA, there's no single universal range — different methods and labs report different numbers. That's why cystatin C has been slower to become a first-line standard, even though its physiology is solid.

Cystatin C can also be influenced by thyroid disease, glucocorticoid therapy, and some systemic inflammatory states — factors worth keeping in mind when interpreting a borderline value.

---

## Head-to-Head

| Criterion | SDMA | Cystatin C |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence base | Strong, extensive | Moderate, growing |
| IRIS staging standard? | ✅ Yes | ❌ No |
| Universal reference interval? | ✅ 0–14 µg/dL | ❌ Assay-dependent |
| Muscle-mass independent | ✅ | ✅ |
| Rises early (~25–40% loss) | ✅ | ✅ |
| Confounders | Minimal | Thyroid, steroids, inflammation |
| Best role | First-line + staging | Confirmatory / second opinion |

The honest summary: **SDMA is the better first-line marker today**, largely because of the IRIS staging system built around it and its universal reference range. Cystatin C isn't inferior — it's just less standardized. Where cystatin C shines is as an *independent* check: when SDMA is borderline, when renal status is muddied by other disease, or when you want a second data point before committing a patient to a CKD diagnosis.

---

## IRIS Staging: Where SDMA Fits

The International Renal Interest Society (IRIS) staging system is the global standard for chronic kidney disease. It uses creatinine and SDMA together:

| IRIS Stage | Dog Creatinine (mg/dL) | Cat Creatinine (mg/dL) | SDMA (µg/dL) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stage 1 | <1.4 | <1.6 | <18 |
| Stage 2 | 1.4–2.8 | 1.6–2.8 | 18–35 |
| Stage 3 | 2.9–5.0 | 2.9–5.0 | 36–54 |
| Stage 4 | >5.0 | >5.0 | >54 |

The clinical power here is Stage 1. A patient with a normal creatinine but an SDMA of 15–17 µg/dL is non-azotemic but has reduced GFR — early CKD that you can act on with diet, blood-pressure control, and monitoring. That's a patient you'd have missed entirely with creatinine alone.

---

## Sample Handling and Confounders

- **SDMA** is robust — minimally affected by muscle mass and holding up well, though severe hemolysis or lipemia can still interfere with fluorescence detection.
- **Cystatin C** has more confounders. Thyroid disease, glucocorticoid therapy, and some systemic inflammatory states can shift cystatin C independent of kidney function — one reason to pair it with SDMA rather than rely on it alone.
- **Both markers** use serum or plasma; follow the kit insert for anticoagulant and stability guidance.

The pairing is the point: two independent markers, two different confounders, one confident answer.

## A Practical Decision Guide

| Scenario | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Routine senior screening | SDMA (with creatinine) |
| Suspicious SDMA, want confirmation | Add cystatin C |
| Muscle-wasted patient | SDMA or cystatin C (not creatinine alone) |
| Monitoring diagnosed CKD | SDMA serially (IRIS staging) |
| Azotemic + multiple comorbidities | SDMA for staging, cystatin C as independent check |
| Pre-anesthesia / pre-nephrotoxic drug | SDMA |

## Key Takeaways

1. Creatinine is a **late, muscle-mass-dependent** marker — it misses early CKD and fails in cachectic patients.
2. SDMA and cystatin C both **rise at ~25–40% function loss** and are **muscle-mass independent**.
3. SDMA (0–14 µg/dL) is the **IRIS staging standard** and the better first-line choice; a persistent >14 µg/dL supports Stage 1 CKD.
4. Cystatin C (~0.5–1.2 mg/L dog, 0.6–1.8 mg/L cat) is a **strong independent confirmatory marker**, though assay-dependent.
5. **Use SDMA first, add cystatin C to confirm or clarify** — and track serial values, because the trend is the diagnosis.
