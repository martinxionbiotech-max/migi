> **TL;DR:** For breeding timing, "progesterone is present" is a useless answer — every cycling bitch has progesterone. The number is the decision: ~2–3 ng/mL is the LH surge, ~5 ng/mL is ovulation, 8–15 ng/mL is the breeding window. Qualitative strips can't distinguish these, and semi-quantitative kits are often capped at 5 ng/mL — blind exactly where frozen-semen precision is needed. This is the one biomarker where quantitative is genuinely non-negotiable.

Progesterone timing is where the quantitative-versus-qualitative debate stops being academic and becomes about whether you get a litter or not. And the answer is not close.

## Why "Positive" Is a Useless Answer

Here's the problem with a qualitative progesterone test — a strip that says "progesterone present":

**Every cycling bitch has progesterone.**

Progesterone is present during proestrus, oestrus, diestrus and pregnancy. A test that answers "is progesterone there?" answers a question nobody was asking. The moment it returns "positive," you have learned exactly nothing about when to breed.

What you actually need is *how much*, because the quantity maps directly to the decision:

| Progesterone (ng/mL) | Decision |
|---|---|
| <1 | Too early |
| 2–3 | LH surge — increase testing frequency |
| ~5 | Ovulation |
| 8–15 | Optimal breeding window |
| >20 | Late — may need TCI or surgical insemination |

The gap between 2 ng/mL and 8 ng/mL is the gap between "not yet" and "breed now." A qualitative strip cannot cross that gap. Only a number can.

## The Semi-Quantitative Trap

Between "positive/negative" and a true number sits the semi-quantitative kit — the one that gives broad colour bands like "low," "medium," "high." It feels like a compromise: cheaper than a quantitative analyzer, more informative than a bare yes/no.

The peer-reviewed literature is blunt. A 2021 study comparing progesterone quantification methods concluded that semi-quantitative methods are **significantly less accurate** than quantitative measurement — and, more damningly, that their usefulness for cryopreserved-semen work is "considerably reduced" because their measurement range is generally **restricted to 5 ng/mL** (PMC8924395).

Why does that 5 ng/mL ceiling matter so much? Because frozen-semen insemination targets the early post-ovulatory period, when progesterone runs **10–20 ng/mL**. A kit that can't read above 5 ng/mL is blind precisely in the range you're trying to hit.

## The Frozen-Semen Case: Where It's Truly Non-Negotiable

Let's make the stakes concrete. Frozen semen survives roughly **12 hours** in the bitch — versus 4–6 days for fresh semen. That 12-hour window means you have to hit the fertile period almost exactly.

The target for frozen-semen insemination is the early post-ovulatory window — progesterone **10–20 ng/mL**. To land there:

- You need a number, not a band.
- You need a number *above 5 ng/mL* — which is exactly where semi-quantitative kits stop.
- You need it *fast*, because the cycle doesn't pause while you wait for a reference lab.

This is the clearest case in all of small-animal diagnostics for a quantitative in-house method. A fluorescence immunoassay analyzer returns the actual ng/mL value in minutes, on the same day — which is the only way to time a frozen-semen insemination precisely.

## The Methods That Deliver a Real Number

For completeness, the quantitative methods used for canine progesterone include:

- **Radioimmunoassay (RIA)** — the classic gold standard, now mostly a reference-lab method.
- **LC-MS/MS** — highest specificity, used as a calibration reference by some manufacturers.
- **Chemiluminescence immunoassay (CLIA)** — common on reference-lab platforms.
- **Enzyme-linked fluorescence assay / fluorescence immunoassay (FIA)** — the in-house, on-site option that returns a number in minutes.

Each has a role. The in-house FIA option earns its place precisely where speed matters — a fast-moving cycle that can't wait for a 24–72 hour send-out.

## When a Qualitative Strip Is Fine

To be fair: there is one narrow place a qualitative or semi-quantitative kit is acceptable. For a **fresh-semen natural breeding**, where the timing tolerance is measured in days, a broad-band home kit can indicate *when to schedule a veterinary blood draw*. It's a triage tool, not a timing tool.

But for the actual decision — chilled or frozen semen, or any program where a missed window is costly — a quantitative number is the only defensible choice.

## Application & Commercial Angle

Who should care: breeders and clinics timing ovulation, especially with chilled or frozen semen. The number is what separates a usable breeding window from a positive strip.

In practice, quantitative progesterone supports the high-value cases — frozen-semen precision and repeat breeding clients — which is where the analyzer's commercial case lives.

## FAQ

### What is the difference between quantitative and qualitative progesterone?

**2 readouts, 1 decision**: a qualitative test answers 'is progesterone present?' with yes/no; a quantitative test measures the actual ng/mL concentration. For breeding, the concentration is the decision — 2 ng/mL means 'not yet,' 5 ng/mL means ovulation, 8–15 ng/mL means breed.

### Why is a qualitative progesterone strip useless for breeding timing?

Because **1 cycling bitch always has measurable progesterone** — 'positive' tells you nothing you didn't already know. You need the number: **2 vs 8 ng/mL** is the difference between too early and the optimal window.

### What is the problem with semi-quantitative progesterone kits?

Because **capped at 5 ng/mL**, semi-quantitative kits are blind exactly where frozen-semen timing requires **10–20 ng/mL**. They give broad bands (low/medium/high) instead of a precise number and are less accurate.

### Which progesterone level is the breeding window?

Ovulation is ~4–10 ng/mL (many bitches ~5 ng/mL), and the optimal breeding window is 8–15 ng/mL. Peak fertile period can run to 15–40 ng/mL. These distinctions all require a quantitative number.

### Can I use a home progesterone test kit?

For **1 semen type**, yes: fresh-semen natural breeding can use a broad-band kit to decide when to schedule a veterinary blood draw. For chilled or frozen semen, home kits lack the precision needed.

### What methods give quantitative progesterone?

**4 quantitative methods** return a number: RIA, LC-MS/MS, CLIA, and enzyme-linked fluorescence assay — including in-house FIA analyzers that return results in minutes.

## Key Takeaways

1. **1 answer is useless** — "progesterone present" tells you nothing, because every cycling bitch has progesterone.
2. The number is the decision: 2–3 ng/mL (LH surge), ~5 ng/mL (ovulation), 8–15 ng/mL (breed).
3. Semi-quantitative kits are less accurate and often capped at 5 ng/mL — blind in the 10–20 ng/mL range frozen semen needs.
4. Frozen semen's 12-hour survival window makes quantitative, same-day measurement non-negotiable.
5. For **1 semen type** — fresh-semen natural breeding — a broad-band kit can triage, but the real decision still needs a number.
