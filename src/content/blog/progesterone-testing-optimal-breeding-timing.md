> **TL;DR:** Progesterone timing is a serial measurement, not a single snapshot. The reference points are ~2–3 ng/mL (LH surge), ~5 ng/mL (ovulation), and 8–15 ng/mL (optimal breeding window). Test every 1–3 days, watch the rate of rise — a slow climber with frozen semen averages fewer puppies — and remember that a quantitative number, not a strip, is what pins down a window measured in hours.

Progesterone timing is the foundation of canine breeding, and it's deceptively simple: measure the hormone, watch it rise, breed at the right moment. The simplicity hides the precision that separates a full litter from a missed cycle.

## The Progesterone Timeline

Progesterone rises through the canine cycle in a predictable pattern. The reference points, drawn from large-scale clinical studies and specialist guidance:

| Progesterone | Clinical meaning |
|---|---|
| <1 ng/mL | Baseline — too early |
| 1.5–2.0 ng/mL | Initial rise, coinciding with the LH surge |
| 2.0–3.0 ng/mL | LH surge (a large-scale study found a mean of 2.7 ng/mL) |
| 4–10 ng/mL | Ovulation (many bitches ~5 ng/mL) |
| 8–15 ng/mL | Optimal breeding window |
| >20 ng/mL | Late fertile period — may need TCI or surgical insemination |

Two timing facts anchor the whole protocol:

1. Ovulation follows the LH surge by roughly **48–72 hours**.
2. The bitch ovulates *immature* oocytes that need another 48–72 hours to mature — so the true fertile window is **four to six days after the LH surge**.

## The Serial Protocol

Because the rise is gradual, a single measurement is nearly useless — you need the trend. The standard protocol:

- Start around **day 5 of heat**, or when vaginal cytology shows the onset of oestrus.
- Test every **1–3 days**.
- Increase frequency to **daily** as the rise accelerates.

The reason for daily testing late in the cycle: once progesterone passes ~4 ng/mL, a **24-hour jump of 3–4 ng/mL or more** confirms ovulation. Some bitches jump as much as 8–10 ng/mL in a day. If you're only testing every three days, you can sail right past the window and never know.

## The Slow-Rise Trap

Some bitches stall — sitting at 2.5–4.0 ng/mL for several days before continuing to climb. This "stall" is a genuine clinical phenomenon, and it's where naive timing goes wrong.

The stakes of a slow rise were quantified in a study of over **1,400 breeding cycles**: a slow progesterone rise paired with frozen semen produced an average of **3.9 puppies**, versus **5.6** with a fast rise.

The takeaway for practice: track the *rate* of rise, not just the absolute value. A bitch whose progesterone climbs slowly may need a different insemination plan — or a switch from frozen to a more forgiving semen type — than one that surges.

## Semen Type Sets the Precision Bar

Why all this precision? Because the type of semen dictates how forgiving your timing can afford to be:

| Semen type | Survival in the bitch | Timing tolerance |
|---|---|---|
| Fresh | 4–6 days | Forgiving — a day off still works |
| Chilled | ~3 days | Moderate |
| Frozen | ~12 hours | Unforgiving — near-exact timing required |

For a natural breeding with fresh semen, an approximate window is fine. For frozen semen — with a 12-hour survival window and a target of 10–20 ng/mL — only a precise quantitative number will do.

## The Unit Trap

Different analyzers and labs report progesterone in different units, and a misread unit is a misread cycle. The conversion:

```
ng/mL × 3.18 = nmol/L
nmol/L ÷ 3.18 = ng/mL
```

Before you interpret any value, confirm which unit it's in. A "5" in ng/mL means ovulation; a "5" in nmol/L means roughly 1.6 ng/mL — still early. The distinction is the difference between breeding today and breeding next week.

## Why Quantitative Beats Qualitative Here

Progesterone timing is the cleanest demonstration of why quantitative matters. The decisions hinge on distinctions of 2–3 ng/mL:

- 2 ng/mL → "not yet"
- 5 ng/mL → "ovulation"
- 8 ng/mL → "breed now"

A colour-change strip cannot make these distinctions. A semi-quantitative kit gives broad bands (low/medium/high) — and, critically, is often **capped at 5 ng/mL**, which the literature notes makes it "considerably reduced" in usefulness for frozen-semen work, where you need to read 10–20 ng/mL (PMC8924395).

An in-house quantitative immunoassay analyzer returns the actual number in minutes, letting you time insemination the same day instead of waiting 24–72 hours for a reference lab — a delay that can miss a fast-moving window entirely.

## FAQ

### What progesterone level indicates ovulation in dogs?

Ovulation occurs when progesterone reaches roughly 4–10 ng/mL, with many bitches ovulating around 5 ng/mL. This follows the LH surge, when progesterone first rises to about 2–3 ng/mL, by roughly 48–72 hours.

### What progesterone level is the optimal breeding window?

The optimal breeding window is commonly placed at 8–15 ng/mL, roughly four to six days after the LH surge, once the ovulated oocytes have matured. Some sources extend the fertile period to 15–40 ng/mL at peak.

### How often should I test progesterone during a cycle?

Start around day 5 of heat and test every 1–3 days, increasing frequency as the rise approaches. Once progesterone passes ~4 ng/mL, a 24-hour jump of 3–4 ng/mL or more confirms ovulation, so daily testing becomes valuable at that point.

### Does the rate of progesterone rise matter?

Yes. Research on over 1,400 cycles found that a slow rise with frozen semen produced an average of 3.9 puppies versus 5.6 with a fast rise. Track both the value and the rate of change.

### How do I convert progesterone units?

ng/mL × 3.18 = nmol/L, and nmol/L ÷ 3.18 = ng/mL. Always confirm which unit your analyzer or lab reports before interpreting a value.

### Can I time breeding with a semi-quantitative progesterone kit?

For fresh-semen natural breeding, a broad-band kit may be enough to schedule a veterinary blood draw. For chilled or frozen semen, semi-quantitative kits are inadequate — their measurement range is often capped at 5 ng/mL, while frozen-semen timing requires 10–20 ng/mL.

## Key Takeaways

1. Progesterone timing is a serial trend, not a snapshot — test every 1–3 days, daily late in the cycle.
2. Reference points: LH surge ~2–3 ng/mL, ovulation ~5 ng/mL, optimal window 8–15 ng/mL.
3. A 24-hour jump of 3–4 ng/mL after ~4 ng/mL confirms ovulation.
4. The rate of rise predicts litter size — a slow rise with frozen semen averaged 3.9 puppies vs 5.6.
5. Confirm units (ng/mL vs nmol/L) and use a quantitative method — semi-quantitative kits cap at 5 ng/mL.
