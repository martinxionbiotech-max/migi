> **TL;DR** — The reason ovulation timing is an obsession in canine AI is simple: **semen lifespan varies by 100-fold** — fresh semen survives **4–6 days**, chilled ~**3 days**, but frozen only ~**12 hours**. Natural mating shrugs off a day of error; frozen-semen AI does not. The map is: LH surge at ~**2–3 ng/mL**, ovulation at ~**4–10 ng/mL** (≈5), oocytes mature over the next **2–3 days**, optimal breeding window **8–15 ng/mL**, and frozen-semen insemination targeting **10–20 ng/mL**. The payoff is concrete: a 1,400-cycle study found a **1.7-puppy difference per litter** (5.6 vs 3.9) between correctly and mistimed frozen-semen breedings.

---

## In Plain Terms

Think of semen like a perishable delivery. Fresh semen is a same-week courier — it can wait. Chilled semen is overnight shipping — tight but manageable. Frozen semen is a live organ transplant — it has hours, not days. The shorter the shelf life, the more precisely you have to know the moment the egg is ready. That moment is what progesterone measures.

---

## The Semen-Survival Problem

| Semen type | Survival in the bitch | Timing tolerance |
|---|---|---|
| Fresh | 4–6 days | Forgiving — a day early still works |
| Chilled | ~3 days | Moderate |
| Frozen | **~12 hours** | Unforgiving — hours matter |

This single table is why the whole discipline exists. If you only ever breed fresh, you can be casual. The moment you use frozen semen — which is how most imported genetics travel — precision becomes the entire game.

---

## The Ovulation Map, in Numbers

Progesterone rises in a predictable wave, and each number maps to a decision:

| Progesterone (ng/mL) | Event | Action |
|---|---|---|
| < 1 | Too early | Retest in 1–2 days |
| 2–3 | LH surge | Switch to daily testing |
| 4–10 | Ovulation | Note date — oocytes mature over next 2–3 days |
| 8–15 | **Breeding window** | Natural / fresh-semen breeding |
| 10–20 | **Frozen-semen window** | Inseminate now |
| > 20 | Late | Consider surgical/TCI insemination |

The subtlety that trips people: **ovulation (4–10 ng/mL) is not the breeding moment.** The oocytes need 2–3 days to mature after ovulation before they can be fertilised, which is why the breeding window sits *above* the ovulation number, at 8–15 ng/mL.

---

## The Frozen-Semen Precision Problem

Frozen semen lives ~12 hours, so you need to land insemination in a narrow window. That window — 10–20 ng/mL — sits **above the ceiling of most semi-quantitative strips**, which typically cap around 5 ng/mL. The result: a strip tells you "progesterone present," which is true of every cycling bitch, but cannot see the number that decides the hour.

This is the concrete reason frozen-semen programmes require a **quantitative** reader. It is not marketing — it is arithmetic: a tool that cannot measure 10 ng/mL cannot time a 12-hour window.

---

## The Payoff, Quantified

A 1,400-cycle study put a number on correct timing: correctly timed frozen-semen breedings produced a mean of **5.6 puppies**, mistimed ones **3.9** — a **1.7-puppy difference per litter**. Across a breeding programme, that gap compounds into a lot of puppies, and in purebred terms, a lot of revenue. One missed insemination also wastes the entire cost of the frozen straw, the import, and the cycle.

---

## The Testing Cadence

1. Begin progesterone testing a few days into oestrus (or at first acceptance of the male).
2. Test every 1–2 days until you catch the surge (~2–3 ng/mL).
3. Switch to daily once the rise begins — the 2→8 ng/mL climb can happen inside 48 hours.
4. Inseminate per the semen table: fresh at 8–15, frozen at 10–20 ng/mL.
5. Confirm pregnancy with relaxin (~day 28–30) or ultrasound.

## FAQ

### Why does timing matter more for AI?

Semen lifespan spans **4–6 days (fresh) to ~12 hours (frozen)**. Natural mating is forgiving; frozen-semen AI is a narrow-window precision exercise.

### What progesterone marks ovulation?

LH surge ~**2–3**, ovulation ~**4–10** ng/mL (≈5), oocytes mature **2–3** more days, breeding window **8–15**, frozen-semen target **10–20**.

### How often to test?

Every **1–2 days**, daily once movement is seen — the **2→8 ng/mL** rise can happen in **48 hours**.

### Fresh vs chilled vs frozen timing?

Fresh (**4–6 days**) is forgiving, chilled (~**3 days**) moderate, frozen (~**12 hours**) unforgiving. Shorter lifespan = more precision needed.

### Why can't a strip do the job?

Strips cap at **5 ng/mL**, blind exactly where frozen-semen timing lives (**10–20 ng/mL**). They can't measure the number that decides the hour.

### What's the payoff of correct timing?

A **1,400-cycle** study found a **1.7-puppy difference per litter** (5.6 vs 3.9) between correct and mistimed frozen-semen breedings.

## Key Takeaways

1. **Semen lifespan drives the whole discipline** — fresh **4–6 days**, chilled ~**3 days**, frozen ~**12 hours**. The shorter the life, the tighter the window.
2. **Ovulation is not the breeding moment** — oocytes mature **2–3 days** after ovulation, so the breeding window (8–15 ng/mL) sits *above* the ovulation number (4–10).
3. **Frozen semen is a quantitative-only game** — its **10–20 ng/mL** window is above the **5 ng/mL** ceiling of strips.
4. **Test daily once the surge is caught** — the **2→8 ng/mL** climb fits inside **48 hours**.
