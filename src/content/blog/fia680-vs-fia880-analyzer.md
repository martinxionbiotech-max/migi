> **TL;DR:** The FIA680 and FIA880 are the same quantitative fluorescence engine in **2 body styles**: **1 channel** vs **6 channels**. Throughput splits to **200 vs 300 tests/hour**, and the FIA880 adds constant-temperature control to keep **CV below 10%** under load. The deciding variable isn't total volume—it's how many tests must run at the same time.

When a practice moves to quantitative point-of-care testing, one of the first practical decisions is which analyzer to choose. Migibio's two readers — the **FIA680** and the **FIA880** — share the same core technology but are built for different volumes and workflows. The choice is less about "which is better" and more about "which matches how my clinic actually operates."

## The Same Foundation

Both analyzers are built on fluorescence immunochromatographic technology (FICT) and share the same performance DNA:

- **Quantitative results** — a numerical concentration, not a line
- **Sensitivity** down to picogram-per-millilitre level (LOD)
- **Precision** with a coefficient of variation below 10%
- **Multilingual interface** — Chinese, English, Russian, Italian
- **Cloud standard curves** and online software updates
- **Built-in thermal printer** for instant results

They also run the same reagent menu — the full range of canine and feline biomarkers. The difference between them is entirely about throughput and workflow.

## FIA680: Single-Channel, Economical, Portable

The FIA680 is a **single-channel** analyzer. It processes one sample at a time and reports a result in **3–11 minutes**, with throughput up to **200 tests per hour**.

Its character:

- Compact, portable footprint
- Lower acquisition cost
- Three-step operation, minimal training
- Wi-Fi connectivity for cloud standard-curve download
- Cloud chip technology — recover lost ID chips over Wi-Fi

The FIA680 is the natural fit for a **small clinic, a solo practitioner, or a mobile vet** where testing is sequential — one patient, one test, one result, then the next. When demand is modest and concurrency is not an issue, a single channel is simply the economical choice.

## FIA880: Six-Channel, High-Throughput

The FIA880 has **six independent channels**, running **six samples simultaneously** with throughput up to **300 tests per hour** and results in **3–15 minutes**.

Its distinguishing features:

- Six parallel channels — no queuing between users
- **Constant-temperature control** for temperature-sensitive reactions
- Larger benchtop footprint
- Thermal printer plus A4 report export for hospital branding

The FIA880 is built for a **medium-to-large hospital or reference laboratory** where several clinicians run tests at the same time.

## Why Constant-Temperature Control Matters

This is the one genuine technical differentiator between the two instruments, and it deserves more than a bullet point.

Fluorescence immunochromatographic reactions are temperature-sensitive: binding kinetics and signal intensity vary with temperature, and that variation can shift the measured concentration. In a busy lab running continuous tests, ambient temperature swings are a real source of run-to-run drift.

The FIA880's constant-temperature incubation holds the reaction at an optimal temperature. That improves lot-to-lot reproducibility and helps keep the CV below 10% even under heavy, sustained use. It's not a marketing feature — it's a precision feature, and it's why the six-channel instrument is the right one when measurement stability under load is the priority.

## The Decision That Actually Matters: Concurrency

The key variable is not total daily volume — it is whether tests happen **at the same time**.

A practice that runs 100 tests a day, all spread out across the day, may be perfectly served by a single-channel analyzer. A practice that runs 80 tests a day but with three clinicians all needing results during a busy morning rush will hit a bottleneck on a single channel — every sample waits in line.

The six-channel design removes that queue. If your workflow has simultaneous demand, that is your signal.

## Application & Commercial Angle

Who should care: buyers choosing between a single-channel and six-channel analyzer. The decision is concurrency and workflow, not test quality.

In practice, the FIA680 suits lower-volume, sequential and mobile use; the FIA880 suits high-volume concurrent testing. Match the channel count to real peak-hour demand to avoid paying for idle capacity.

## FAQ

### What is the difference between the FIA680 and FIA880?

**1 channel vs 6 channels**: the FIA680 runs one sample at a time for small clinics; the FIA880 runs six samples simultaneously for hospitals and reference laboratories with concurrent volume.

### Which analyzer has higher throughput?

The FIA880 does **up to 300 tests/hour** across six channels; the FIA680 does **up to 200 tests/hour** on one channel. Both deliver quantitative results in minutes with **CV below 10%**.

### How do I decide between single-channel and six-channel?

Choose on concurrent demand, not total volume: if multiple clinicians test at once, **6 channels** remove the bottleneck; otherwise **1 channel** is the more economical choice.

### Why does the FIA880 include constant-temperature control?

Because temperature shifts binding kinetics and signal intensity, constant-temperature incubation improves reproducibility and helps keep **CV below 10%** under heavy use.

### Do both analyzers run the same tests?

Yes — both run the same **1 reagent menu** (infectious disease antigens/antibodies, hormones, inflammatory and organ biomarkers). The difference is throughput and workflow, not test availability.

### Which analyzer is better for a mobile or small practice?

The FIA680 — **1 channel**, compact and portable, with lower acquisition cost and 3-step operation. For sequential point-of-care testing, a single channel is the economical choice.
