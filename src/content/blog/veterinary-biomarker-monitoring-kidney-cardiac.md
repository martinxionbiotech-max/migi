Infectious-disease testing answers "is the pathogen present?" — but a large share of veterinary illness is **non-infectious**, driven by organ dysfunction, inflammation, and metabolic disease. For these conditions, **quantitative biomarkers** are the diagnostic key. This guide covers the major biomarkers measured in modern veterinary practice and how point-of-care testing has transformed their use.

## Why Biomarkers Matter

A biomarker is any measurable substance whose level reflects a biological or disease state. Unlike clinical signs, which appear late, biomarkers often change **early** in disease — sometimes before the animal shows any outward symptoms. Quantitative, serial biomarker measurement therefore enables:

- **Earlier diagnosis** — detecting disease in its reversible phase.
- **Objective staging** — quantifying severity.
- **Treatment monitoring** — tracking whether therapy is working.
- **Prognostication** — predicting outcomes.

## Renal Biomarkers

### SDMA (Symmetric Dimethylarginine)

SDMA is a by-product of protein metabolism excreted almost entirely by the kidneys. Its defining advantage is that it rises **earlier** than creatinine — when as little as 25–40% of kidney function is lost, versus 60–70% for creatinine.

- **Detects** chronic kidney disease (CKD) earlier.
- **Independent of muscle mass** — unlike creatinine, not confounded by cachexia or muscle wasting.
- **Supports IRIS staging** of CKD.

For a full treatment, see [Early Detection of Chronic Kidney Disease: The Role of SDMA](/blog/early-detection-chronic-kidney-disease-sdma-dogs-cats) and [What Is SDMA in Cats?](/blog/what-is-cats-sdma-how-to-diagnose-it-quickly).

### Cystatin C

Cystatin C is another early renal marker, produced at a constant rate and filtered by the glomerulus. Like SDMA, it rises before creatinine and is less influenced by muscle mass. Cystatin C is available for both dogs and cats.

## Cardiac Biomarkers

### NT-proBNP (N-Terminal Pro-B-Type Natriuretic Peptide)

When the heart muscle is stretched or stressed — as in congestive heart failure or significant cardiac disease — it releases B-type natriuretic peptide. The stable N-terminal fragment, NT-proBNP, is measurable in blood.

- **Screens for occult cardiac disease** before clinical signs appear.
- **Distinguishes cardiac from respiratory causes** of dyspnoea and cough.
- **Monitors heart-failure therapy.**

NT-proBNP is especially valuable in breeds predisposed to heart disease (e.g., Cavalier King Charles Spaniels with mitral valve disease) and in cats, where heart disease is often silent.

## Pancreatic Biomarkers

### Pancreatic Lipase (fPL / cPL)

Pancreatic lipase is specific to the pancreas and rises during pancreatitis. Species-specific assays (feline pancreatic lipase, fPL; canine pancreatic lipase, cPL) provide much higher diagnostic accuracy than older amylase/lipase measurements.

- **Diagnoses pancreatitis** with high sensitivity and specificity.
- **Monitors disease severity** and recovery.

## Inflammatory Biomarkers

### C-Reactive Protein (CRP)

CRP is a major acute-phase protein in dogs. It rises rapidly during inflammation and infection and falls just as rapidly with successful treatment.

- **Detects and quantifies systemic inflammation.**
- **Monitors response to therapy** — a falling CRP confirms treatment is working.
- **Distinguishes inflammatory from non-inflammatory disease.**

### Serum Amyloid A (SAA)

SAA is the major acute-phase protein in cats (and also rises in dogs). It increases dramatically within hours of an inflammatory stimulus.

- **Early, sensitive marker of inflammation** in cats.
- **Monitors treatment response.**

## Thyroid Biomarkers

### Total Thyroxine (T4) and TSH

Thyroid hormones regulate metabolism. Hypothyroidism (low T4, high TSH) is common in dogs; hyperthyroidism (high T4) is common in older cats.

- **T4** — primary thyroid hormone measurement.
- **TSH** — pituitary hormone; elevated in canine hypothyroidism.
- **Combined T4 + TSH** — improves diagnostic accuracy for hypothyroidism.

## Reproductive Biomarkers

### Progesterone

Progesterone measurement is essential for canine breeding management — determining the optimal mating or insemination time.

- **Tracks the oestrous cycle.**
- **Times ovulation** for optimal breeding or artificial insemination.
- **Predicts whelping** date.

### Relaxin

Relaxin is produced by the placenta and is a reliable **pregnancy** marker in dogs.

- **Confirms pregnancy** from around day 21–28 post-ovulation.
- **Distinguishes true pregnancy from pseudopregnancy.**

## The Power of Serial Testing

A single biomarker value is informative; a **trend** is transformative. Serial measurement reveals whether disease is progressing or responding to treatment:

- A rising SDMA signals advancing kidney disease; a stabilising SDMA confirms therapy is working.
- A falling CRP confirms infection is resolving.
- A rising progesterone pinpoints the exact day to breed.

Point-of-care quantitative testing makes this serial monitoring practical — results in minutes, at the cage-side, without the delay and cost of repeated laboratory submissions.

## Conclusion

Quantitative biomarkers have moved veterinary medicine from reactive, sign-based diagnosis to proactive, early detection. By measuring the right biomarker at the right time — and tracking it over time — clinicians can diagnose earlier, treat more effectively, and give owners clearer answers.
