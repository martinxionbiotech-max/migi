> **TL;DR** — Choosing a supplier comes down to **10 questions across 5 areas**: performance, certifications, manufacturing quality, logistics, and the relationship. The single most important is **#1: will you share your analytical performance data?** A supplier that hesitates on that is telling you everything you need to know.

---

## Why the Right Questions Matter

Here's the uncomfortable pattern in this industry: most supplier problems aren't surprises. They're the result of questions that were never asked. A distributor signs with a factory based on a brochure and a good price, and nine months later discovers the lead times were never guaranteed, the cold-chain items spoil in transit, and the "CE certificate" doesn't cover their product line.

The fix is a due-diligence list. Ten questions. If you get straight answers to all ten, you've done more homework than 90% of buyers in this market.

---

## In Plain Terms

Choosing a supplier is like vetting a business partner before going into business together. You wouldn't sign a partnership without asking about their finances, their track record, and what happens when things go wrong. A test kit supplier is the same — you're tying your revenue to their consistency for years. Ask the questions up front, because they get a lot harder to ask after the contract is signed.

---

## The Ten Questions

### 1. Will you share your analytical performance data?

The most important question on this list. Ask for **sensitivity (detection limit)** and **precision (CV%)** for the analytes you'll actually sell. A reputable factory publishes this. A factory that deflects — "our quality is very good" — is a red flag.

**Good answer:** CV% numbers by analyte, detection limits in pg/mL or ng/mL, a validation report.

### 2. What certifications do you hold — and what's the exact scope?

Don't accept "ISO certified." Ask for the certificate number and the **scope**. A certificate that covers one product line doesn't cover another. Check it's current and covers the products you're buying.

**Good answer:** Certificate numbers, scopes, and issuing bodies you can verify. See [Veterinary Diagnostic Certifications: CE, ISO, and QC Explained](/blog/veterinary-diagnostic-certifications-ce-iso-qc).

### 3. Can you trace every kit back to its raw-material lot?

This is the recall-protection question. If a batch has a problem, can the factory identify every affected kit? **Batch traceability** is a GMP fundamental — and it's what separates a real manufacturer from a repackager.

**Good answer:** Yes, with lot numbers on every kit and a documented traceability system.

### 4. What are your storage requirements — and is anything cold-chain?

This quietly drives your logistics cost. **Room-temperature reagents** mean cheaper shipping, no cold-chain failure points, and less spoilage. If six of a factory's analytes need refrigeration, that's six ways for a shipment to go wrong.

**Good answer:** Room-temperature storage across the menu, with explicit stability data.

### 5. What are your lead times, and are they contractually guaranteed?

A supplier that can't commit to a lead time can't commit to supply. Ask for **documented, guaranteed lead times** for reorders, not just the initial order.

**Good answer:** A written lead time with penalties or terms for slippage.

### 6. What's the MOQ, and how does pricing tier?

MOQ and pricing tiers tell you whether the supplier is set up for your volume. Understand the **analyzer MOQ** and the **reagent MOQ** separately, and how unit cost falls with volume.

**Good answer:** Transparent tiered pricing. See [Bulk Ordering & Distributor Pricing: How It Works](/blog/bulk-ordering-distributor-pricing-veterinary-diagnostics).

### 7. Can you do OEM / private label — and what does it cost?

Even if you start reselling under their brand, confirm the factory can do **private label later**. The branding cost and MOQ should be clear now, not discovered later.

**Good answer:** Clear OEM path, branding cost, and MOQ. See [Veterinary Diagnostic OEM/ODM: What You Need to Know](/blog/veterinary-diagnostic-oem-odm).

### 8. What's your export experience, and to which markets?

Export experience matters because international logistics, customs documentation, and multi-market registration are all separate skills. A factory that's shipped to 30+ countries has solved problems a domestic-only factory hasn't.

**Good answer:** A list of markets, and ideally references in yours.

### 9. What technical support do you provide — and in what language?

After the sale, you'll need training, troubleshooting, and sometimes on-site support. Confirm the **support channel, response time, and language**.

**Good answer:** Dedicated support, your language, defined response times.

### 10. Can I talk to an existing distributor?

References are the cheapest due diligence there is. A factory with happy distributors will connect you. One that can't produce a single reference is telling you something.

**Good answer:** Willingness to provide references, or a trial order to validate yourself.

## The Red Flags, Summarized

| Red Flag | What It Predicts |
|---|---|
| Won't share CV% / detection limits | Performance isn't real |
| "ISO certified" but no scope | Certification is cosmetic |
| No batch traceability | Recall nightmare |
| Several cold-chain analytes | Logistics cost + spoilage |
| Vague lead times | Supply disruptions |
| No OEM path | You'll be stuck reselling forever |
| Silent after first inquiry | Poor support |

## How to Use This List

Don't fire all ten questions in one email and wait. Use them as a **scorecard** during evaluation:

1. Ask the first three (performance, certifications, traceability) — they're the disqualifiers.
2. If they pass, ask four through nine (logistics, OEM, support).
3. Ask for a reference or a trial order to close.

A supplier that answers all ten cleanly is rare and worth a relationship. Most will stumble somewhere — and *where* they stumble tells you where the problems will be.

## Application & Commercial Angle

Who should care: distributors, importers, clinic groups and private-label brands qualifying a supplier. The buyer perspective is process verification, not sales promises.

The practical worth is avoiding supplier risk — ask for performance data, certifications, batch traceability, lead times and OEM capability before committing volume. Pricing should be quoted against volume, region and OEM terms.

## Key Takeaways

1. The **#1 question** is "will you share your analytical performance data?" — hesitation is disqualifying.
2. Verify **certifications by scope, not certificate count** — 1 certificate with the wrong scope is still a gap.
3. Demand **batch traceability and room-temperature storage** — these 2 things protect you from recalls and logistics costs.
4. **Guaranteed lead times and OEM capability** are the 2 markers separating a supplier from a partner.
5. Use the **10 questions as a scorecard**, not a questionnaire — where a supplier stumbles predicts where problems will be.
