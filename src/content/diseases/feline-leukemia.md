faq:
  - q: "What is the difference between FeLV and FIV?"
    a: "Feline leukaemia virus (FeLV) and feline immunodeficiency virus (FIV) are both retroviruses but differ in transmission and disease. FeLV spreads readily through saliva and close contact and can cause cancer; FIV spreads mainly through bite wounds and causes immune suppression."
  - q: "How are FeLV and FIV detected?"
    a: "FeLV is typically detected by antigen testing (the p27 core antigen) in blood, while FIV is detected by antibody testing. Quantitative fluorescence assays provide objective results for both, supporting diagnosis and monitoring."
  - q: "When should cats be tested for FeLV and FIV?"
    a: "Testing is recommended for new cats entering a household, sick cats with unexplained illness, cats with bite wounds or outdoor access, and before vaccination. Retrovirus screening is a standard part of preventive feline care."
